PART 2 - STAKEHOLDER PITCH (~6 MINUTES)
===============================================================================

Use this in the room, where you have time to be asked questions. Same story as
Part 1, but with the reasoning shown.


--- 1. OPEN WITH THE NUMBER --------------------------------------------------

  "I want to start with a number that took me a while to believe.

   Households around the world throw away about six hundred and thirty million
   tonnes of food every year. Households - not farms, not supermarkets,
   not restaurants. Us. That's sixty percent of all food waste, and it's about
   a fifth of everything we buy.

   Put it in money. The EPA now puts the cost at about seven hundred and
   twenty-eight dollars per person per year. A family of four throws away
   roughly two thousand nine hundred dollars of groceries a year. About
   fifty-six dollars a week, in the bin.

   And if you care about emissions: if food waste were a country, it would be
   the third-largest emitter on Earth. Behind China and the United States,
   ahead of everybody else. Four times the emissions of aviation."


--- 2. THE TWIST: WHY THE OBVIOUS FIX DOESN'T WORK ----------------------------

  "Now, the obvious response is: people need to be better organised. Track your
   groceries. And a lot of apps have been built on exactly that idea.

   But look at why food actually gets thrown out. It isn't mostly spoilage.
   The two biggest causes are confusion about date labels, and simply
   forgetting what you had. Eighty-eight percent of people throw food out near
   the printed date at least sometimes, even when it's perfectly fine.

   So the failure isn't that people don't know what's in the fridge. It's that
   nobody remembers the pattern. Nobody remembers that you buy spinach with
   good intentions and bin it every single time. Nobody remembers that you
   don't like cilantro, so you quietly skip the recipe and the ingredients rot.

   An inventory list can't fix that, because an inventory list has no memory.
   It's a snapshot. Every week it starts over, exactly like you do."


--- 3. WHAT WE BUILT ---------------------------------------------------------

  "PantryMind is a pantry assistant with a memory that survives the
   conversation.

   You talk to it like a person. It knows what you have and when it expires.
   But the important part is what it keeps: your preferences, your dietary
   constraints, the feedback you give it, and - this is the one that matters -
   your waste patterns.

   Then, weeks later, in a completely new session, it uses them. Unprompted.

   Here's the demo in one sentence: you tell it you don't like cilantro, you
   close the browser, you come back in a brand-new private window, you ask what
   to cook - and it recommends something without cilantro and tells you why it
   left it out.

   That's not a chat history trick. It's not in the same session. It went and
   found that memory because it was relevant."


--- 4. HOW IT ACTUALLY WORKS (PLAIN ENGLISH) ---------------------------------

  "Three steps, every single time you send a message.

   ONE - RECALL. We take what you just said and turn it into a vector: a list
   of numbers that captures the meaning, not the words. Then we search every
   memory we hold about you for the closest matches. That's why 'what should I
   cook' can pull up 'I don't like cilantro' - the words don't overlap at all,
   but the meaning connects.

   TWO - RESPOND. We hand the model three things: your question, the memories
   we just found, and your actual pantry with real expiry dates. It answers
   with all of it in view. And we ask it to say out loud when a memory changed
   its answer - because a recommendation you can't explain is one you won't
   trust.

   THREE - WRITE. After answering, a second, cheaper model reads the exchange
   and asks: was anything here worth remembering? A preference, a constraint,
   a complaint - yes. Small talk - no. Only the durable things become memories.
   That's what stops the memory filling up with noise. And it happens in the
   background, so it never slows your reply down.

   The whole thing is that loop. Recall, respond, remember."


--- 5. WHY THE DATABASE CHOICE MATTERS ---------------------------------------

  "One thing worth calling out for the technical folks in the room.

   Normally, doing this means running two databases: a regular one for your
   pantry and your users, and a separate specialist vector database for the
   memories. Two systems, two sets of credentials, two things to keep in sync,
   and the constant risk they drift apart.

   CockroachDB does both. Our pantry table and our memory vectors live side by
   side, in one database, in one transaction boundary. The memory search is a
   plain SQL query with a distributed vector index behind it.

   For a team our size that's the difference between shipping and not
   shipping."


--- 6. MARKET --------------------------------------------------------------

  "Is there an appetite for this? Demonstrably yes.

   Too Good To Go - which does something much narrower, just surplus food from
   shops - has a hundred and twenty million registered users. Fifteen million
   in the US alone. People will absolutely install an app to waste less food.

   The food-waste app market is around one and a half billion dollars today and
   is forecast to reach about five billion by 2035, growing at roughly thirteen
   percent a year.

   But look at what's actually in that market: inventory trackers, barcode
   scanners, receipt scanners, surplus marketplaces. All of them are better
   snapshots. None of them remember you.

   That's the opening. The moat here isn't the pantry list - anyone can build
   that in a weekend. The moat is the memory. An assistant that has known you
   for six months is genuinely hard to switch away from, because your
   replacement starts from zero and you feel it immediately.

   And the value compounds in the right direction. The longer you use it, the
   better it gets, and the more it saves you."


--- 7. WHAT'S NEXT ---------------------------------------------------------

  "Three things, in order.

   Near term: receipt scanning, so building your pantry isn't typing. That's
   the single biggest drop-off point in every app in this category.

   Middle: turn the waste patterns into a shopping brake. Right now we notice
   you always bin the spinach. The obvious next step is to say so before you
   buy it again - 'you've thrown this out three times, buy the small bag.'

   Longer: households, not individuals. Shared memory across everyone who
   cooks in the same kitchen.

   What we'd want from you is a decision on which of those to fund first, and
   whether this goes to market direct-to-consumer or through a grocery partner
   - because the shopping-brake feature is worth considerably more sitting
   inside a grocery app than beside it."

PART 3 - COCKROACHDB TOOLS WE USED
===============================================================================
[Paste into the Devpost field. Two tools, stated precisely.]


1. DISTRIBUTED VECTOR INDEXING  --  used, on the live request path
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Every durable memory is stored in CockroachDB as a 1024-dimension vector, in
the same database and the same schema as the pantry data - no separate vector
store.

  Table:      memory_vectors
  Column:     embedding VECTOR(1024)      (matches Titan Text Embeddings V2)
  Index:      VECTOR INDEX idx_memory_vec_user
                    (user_id, embedding vector_cosine_ops)
  Distance:   cosine, via the <=> operator
  Cluster:    CockroachDB Cloud, v26.2.5

What the agent actually does with it: on every single chat turn, before
answering, it embeds the user's message and runs a nearest-neighbour search
against that user's memories, taking the top 5 by cosine distance. Those
memories are injected into the model's context, and the similarity score of
each one is surfaced in the UI so the user can see exactly what was recalled
and how strongly it matched. This is the mechanism behind the cross-session
recall in the demo video.

One engineering detail we'd rather volunteer than have found. Our first index
was on the embedding column alone. Because recall is always scoped to a single
user, the WHERE user_id = $1 filter meant a vector index without user_id as a
prefix could not serve the query, and EXPLAIN showed the planner falling back
to a full scan. We added a second index with user_id as the index prefix. The
production recall query now plans as:

    -> vector search
         table: memory_vectors@idx_memory_vec_user
         prefix spans: [user_id]

That is the query the demo runs.


2. ccloud CLI  --  used, for cluster access and connection management
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Three commands, all in scripts/provision.sh, which is committed and runnable:

  ccloud auth whoami                       - confirm the authenticated identity
  ccloud cluster info <cluster-id> -o json - resolve the cluster
  ccloud cluster sql <name> --connection-url
                                           - emit the connection string
                                             non-interactively, which is what
                                             populates DATABASE_URL

To be precise about scope: the cluster itself was created in the CockroachDB
Cloud web console before that script existed. We did not use the CLI to
provision it, and the script says so in its own header comment. We used the CLI
to authenticate, locate the cluster, and pull the connection string into the
app's environment.

A genuine gotcha, in case it's useful to the CockroachDB team: `ccloud cluster
list` returned an empty list for us even with valid access, because it scopes
to the current organization and our login resolved to a different default org.
Direct lookup by cluster ID worked immediately. That workaround is documented
in the script.


TOOLS WE DID NOT USE - stated so our other claims can be trusted
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Managed MCP Server. We configured it - the server definition is committed at
.mcp.json and agent/mcp/cockroach-mcp.json, pointed at our cluster. But the
browser OAuth step was never completed, because our account was never added to
the CockroachDB Cloud organization that owns the cluster, and that invite is
held by our other team member. No query was ever run through it. We are not
claiming this tool. It is tracked as an open issue (#23) in our repo.

Agent Skills. Not used at all. It was never in scope for us.

PART 4 - AWS SERVICES WE USED
===============================================================================
[Paste into the Devpost field.]


AMAZON BEDROCK  --  live, on every request
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Bedrock is the entire intelligence layer. Three models, two APIs:

  Titan Text Embeddings V2 - amazon.titan-embed-text-v2:0 - InvokeModel API
      Converts text into 1024-dimension vectors. Called twice per turn: once
      on the incoming message to search memories, once on a memory summary
      before it is stored. This is what makes semantic recall possible.

  Claude Sonnet 4.5 - us.anthropic.claude-sonnet-4-5-20250929-v1:0 - Converse
      Generates every reply, with recalled memories and live pantry contents
      supplied in context.

  Claude Haiku 4.5 - us.anthropic.claude-haiku-4-5-20251001-v1:0 - Converse
      Runs after each reply and classifies the exchange: is this a preference,
      feedback, an action, or just chatter? Only non-chatter becomes a durable
      memory. Using the smaller, cheaper model for the high-frequency
      classification job is a deliberate cost decision.

Both Claude models are invoked through cross-region inference profiles - the
"us." prefix - because on-demand throughput is not offered for them directly.


AWS LAMBDA, AWS SAM, CLOUDFORMATION  --  deployed and verified, NOT on the
live request path
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
We built and deployed seven Node 22 Lambda functions to us-east-1 through AWS
SAM (CloudFormation stack "pantrymind"): three business-logic functions for
pantry, recipes and shopping-list operations, three Bedrock action-group
adapter functions, and a hello-world. They are verified working by direct
invocation, with CloudWatch Logs as evidence.

They are not serving traffic, and here is the honest reason.

Those Lambdas were designed as action groups for a Bedrock Agent - that was our
tool-calling layer, with OpenAPI schemas written and adapters deployed. Partway
through the build, our CreateAgent call started returning:

    AccessDeniedException: Bedrock Agents is in Maintenance Mode.
    New agent creation is not available for accounts without prior
    service usage.

AWS moved Bedrock Agents ("Classic") into Maintenance Mode on 2026-07-30 and
closed it to any account with no prior Agents usage. There is no exception
process. Our account had never created one, so it was permanently locked out,
regardless of how correct our IAM roles and adapters were - and we had verified
all of those independently before this surfaced. The migration path, Bedrock
AgentCore, is a full re-architecture rather than a swap-in, which was not
realistic in the hours remaining.

So we moved the reasoning to the Bedrock Converse API, and the shipped app
calls CockroachDB directly from its API routes. What we lost was
Bedrock-mediated tool calling; we compensated by loading live pantry contents
straight into the model's context instead. The Lambdas and the OpenAPI action
group definitions remain in the repository as evidence of the work and as the
starting point if we revisit AgentCore.

CloudWatch Logs is in use for Lambda logging. We have not set up custom metrics
or alarms, so we are not claiming observability beyond the default.


AMAZON S3  --  we are not claiming it
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
We provisioned a bucket early for demo assets and then never used it. It is
empty, and no application code reads or writes S3.

The one real S3 involvement is indirect: AWS SAM uploads our bundled Lambda
packages and CloudFormation templates to its managed artifact bucket on every
deploy. That is S3 as a build-artifact store, not a product feature, and we
would rather say that than pad the list.

THE THREE QUESTIONS YOU WILL GET
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q: "Isn't this just a to-do list with extra steps?"

   "A list tells you what you have. It can't tell you that you've bought and
    binned spinach four times. That's the thing that actually changes
    behaviour, and it needs memory across months, not a snapshot. That's the
    whole product."


Q: "Why didn't you use Bedrock Agents? Wasn't that the point?"

   "It was our plan, and we built for it - the action groups and adapters are
    in the repo, deployed and tested. AWS closed Agents to new accounts on
    July 30th, partway through our build, with no exception process. We found
    out when CreateAgent returned an access-denied. Rather than attempt a
    full re-architecture onto AgentCore with hours left, we moved the
    reasoning to the Converse API. The judged behaviour - memory that
    persists and visibly changes recommendations - is fully working. We just
    got there by a different route."


Q: "Does that vector index actually get used, or is it decorative?"

   "Fair question, and it was decorative until we checked. Our first index
    was on the embedding column alone, and because recall is filtered by
    user, EXPLAIN showed a full scan straight past it. We added user_id as
    the index prefix. The production query now plans as a vector search
    against that index, with prefix spans on the user. Happy to run EXPLAIN
    for you."


IF SOMETHING BREAKS ON CAMERA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Recall returns nothing  -> backfill-embeddings.ts wasn't run. Re-run, reload.
  Page is blank           -> `next build` clobbered .next. Stop dev server,
                             rm -rf apps/web/.next, restart.
  Chat is slow            -> first Bedrock call is cold. Send one throwaway
                             message before recording.
